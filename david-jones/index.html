01

Why the brand is dying

Everyone's mum shops there. That is the problem.

David Jones is not dying because of its products. It is dying because its customer base is ageing with it, the department store format is losing to online and specialists, and the brand has had no cultural moment with young Australians in a decade.

Symptom

Ageing core customer

The loyal shopper is 45+. Gen Z sees "DJs" as where their parents buy work shirts and Christmas gifts. No hostility, just no relationship.

Symptom

Format under pressure

Online marketplaces, brand DTC sites and fast fashion take the volume. Department stores keep the overheads. Sales fell 8.7% while costs had to be cut 5.6% just to stay afloat.

Opening

A turnaround is already funded

Vision2025+ ($250M: refurbished flagships, new app, Qantas loyalty partnership) lifted EBITDA 325% in the nine months to March 2026. The new five-year plan, INSPIRE30, is data-led and brand-led. Gen Z is the gap in it.

Say this (45 s)Three facts to remember. One: the brand lost 95% of its value between 2014 and 2023. Two: last financial year it lost $95.5 million. Three: the turnaround is working on costs and loyalty, but every customer they are winning back is one they already had. A legacy brand cannot cost-cut its way to a new generation. That is the strategic gap this campaign fills.

Audience

Generation Z in Australia, 2026

Born 1997 to 2012, so aged 14 to 29 today. Since December 2025 Australia blocks under-16s from social platforms, so the reachable segment is 16 to 29, about 4.8 million Australians. We target the 18 to 26 core: first full-time wage, first "proper" wardrobe, first gifts bought with their own money.

Five audience truths that shape the plan

  • Search starts on TikTok, not Google. If DJ has no native short-form content, it does not exist.
  • Creators are the new shop assistants. Peer recommendation beats brand voice. Paid must amplify people, not polish.
  • Heritage is cool when it is specific. Vintage, archives, "since 1838" are assets if treated like a drop, not a plaque.
  • Secondhand is mainstream. Resale is a value signal and a sustainability signal at the same time.
  • They buy on their terms. Afterpay, app-first, loyalty points they can actually use (Qantas) and physical spaces worth filming.
Say this (60 s)Our audience is 16 to 29, with an 18 to 26 core. I built three personas: Maya the student who thrifts, Tom the first-salary grad, and Priya the micro-creator. Notice none of them hate David Jones. They are indifferent, which is worse. The single most important data point is that 86% of young TikTok users use it as a search engine every week. So when Tom types "first suit Sydney" into TikTok and David Jones is not there, the sale goes to someone else. Everything in the POEM plan is built on these five truths.
03

Campaign strategy

The big idea

Campaign platform

Since 1838.
Yours now.

Flip the weakness into the hook. Being 188 years old is the one thing Zara, Shein and Amazon can never copy. We treat the David Jones archive like a streetwear drop and the flagship like a venue.

  • DJ Archive. Limited reissues of pieces from 188 years of catalogues, co-designed with young Australian labels, dropped monthly, app-only first.
  • DJ Pre-Loved. A curated designer resale floor at Elizabeth St and Bourke St (authenticated, trade-in for points). Resale is how Gen Z enters luxury.
  • The 7th Floor. The flagship top floor becomes a filmable space: creator studio, styling bar, late-night Thursday events.
  • First Wardrobe. A free 30-minute styling session + Qantas points bonus for 18 to 26s. Turns "I'd go if someone told me what to buy" into a booking.
Awareness+25 pts

Acquisition150k

Say this (60 s)The strategy is one platform: "Since 1838. Yours now." The idea is to stop apologising for being old and sell the archive like a drop. Four pillars: the Archive reissues, a Pre-Loved resale floor, the 7th Floor as a creator venue, and a free First Wardrobe styling session with Qantas points. Objectives are SMART: awareness, perception, acquisition and revenue, each with a number and a 12-week or 6-month window. The targets are proposals for the workshop, not David Jones figures.

Media strategy

POEM: Paid, Owned, Earned

Each channel has one job. Paid buys the first look, Owned turns the look into a relationship, Earned makes other people do the selling. Click a circle or a tab.

Paid media is reach we buy: ads, boosted creator content, sponsorships. Job: get the first 3 seconds of attention from people who have never thought about DJ.

Say this (90 s)POEM is the backbone. Paid: we do not run polished brand ads, we buy reach for creator content through TikTok Spark Ads and target the searches Gen Z actually type. Owned: the app gets the drop first for 48 hours, Qantas points are doubled for under-30s, and the store becomes a venue. Earned: the fit-check format, creator organic posting, a PR story the press will want, and the Depop collab. The loop on screen shows how one person moves through all three. Half the budget is paid at launch, but the point is that as Earned grows, Paid can shrink. Owned is where the long-term value sits, because it is capability David Jones keeps.

Execution and measurement

12 weeks, three phases, four scoreboards

Weeks 1 to 3

Tease

  • Archive teaser film, creators show "something found in the DJ basement".
  • App waitlist opens, double Qantas points announced.
  • PR exclusive to one outlet.
Paid-led
Weeks 4 to 6

Launch

  • Drop 01 app-only, façade projection, 7th Floor opening night.
  • Pre-Loved floor opens with Depop trade-in weekend.
  • Spark Ads at full weight, all 12 creators live.
Owned peaks
Weeks 7 to 12

Sustain

  • Drops 02 and 03, Thursday nights every week.
  • Paid shifts to retargeting and UGC amplification.
  • Micro-creator gifting wave, review push.
Earned-led
PaidCPM / CTR

Reach among 18 to 26s, view-through rate on Spark Ads, cost per app install (target under $4).

OwnedAccounts

App installs, under-30 loyalty sign-ups, drop sell-through, styling bookings, email open rate.

EarnedEMV

Earned media value, UGC volume on #DJArchive, share of voice vs Myer, press pieces, review score.

BusinessRevenue

Under-30 share of flagship sales, average basket, repeat visit within 60 days, brand tracker shift.

Say this (45 s)Execution runs 12 weeks: tease, launch, sustain. Each phase is led by a different letter of POEM, which is the whole point of planning media as a system. On measurement, I set one scoreboard per media type plus one for the business, because reach without under-30 revenue would just be an expensive rebrand. The lesson from the Barbeques Galore workshop applies here too: decide the success metrics before the campaign, not after.
